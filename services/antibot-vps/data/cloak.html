On slow mornings

July 28, 2026 · 4 min read

Somewhere between the kettle boiling and the light finding the kitchen table, I've been learning to sit still. Not meditate, exactly — nothing that structured. Just sit, with the tea, and wait for the morning to catch up.

It sounds like a small thing to make a habit of. It hasn't been. My hand keeps drifting toward the phone the way it drifts toward salt at dinner: reflex, not appetite.

Notes from Porto

July 14, 2026 · 6 min read

The building I rented in Porto had a courtyard the size of a good rug, and every morning at seven the woman upstairs would hang laundry over it. Sheets, mostly, and once — memorably — a floor-length yellow dress that swung all day in the breeze.

Portugal is the first country I've been to that felt like it wasn't performing itself. The tiles are old because they're old. The tram runs because people ride it. The fish is fresh because the boat came in this morning. There's no gift shop version of any of it.

The weight of small decisions

June 15, 2026 · 7 min read

My grandmother made the same lunch for forty years. Bread, butter, cheese, an apple. She was not being frugal, exactly, and she was not being lazy. She had, at some point in her twenties, decided what a good lunch looked like, and then never had to make that decision again.

I think about this a lot. Not because I want her lunch, but because I want her clarity. The decisions we make about small things — what to eat, what to wear, when to sleep — quietly determine the weather of a life.
